# What the Lean check establishes about Steinness

**Proof development and verification.** Under Hudson Headley’s direction, OpenAI Codex agents from the GPT-6 family developed the mathematical arguments, wrote the Lean proofs, helped draft the manuscript and ran the internal checks. Headley maintains the release. Lean’s kernel verified the formal declarations. [Contribution record](formalization.yaml)

Lean proves Steinness for every smooth simple conformal metric on the closed unit disk and every open subset of its interior. It proves compactness of holomorphic hulls, separation of distinct points by global holomorphic functions, and existence of two global holomorphic functions giving local coordinates at each point. The second endpoint constructs a complex atlas on the physical domain and proves the three statements for its manifold-holomorphic functions. Both theorems are proved from simplicity. For an abstract surface, the change into these disk coordinates uses published global isothermal parametrization and transport naturality. The public problem listing specifies the two Wirtinger fields defining the complex structure. Lean proves that its real differential equations are equivalent to annihilating precisely those two fields, with the full connection coefficient and both vertical terms. The velocity coordinate has modulus equal to the Riemannian norm. An independent theorem transfers the three Stein conditions under any complex diffeomorphism. ## Identification with Bohr’s question and priority review, 24 November 2026

Bohr asked about the interior of the transport twistor space for every simple surface. In the authors’ definition both the base boundary and the unit-velocity boundary are removed. For an orientation-preserving global isothermal chart `z`, the velocity coordinate of a tangent vector `v` is `μ = exp(σ) dz(v)`. Thus `|μ| = |v|_g` and the full interior is the open bidisk. A compact simple surface is a disk. Global isothermal coordinates are smooth to its boundary, and the resulting smooth conformal factor has a smooth extension to a collar. The isometry preserves boundary convexity, geodesic exit and conjugate points. These are precisely the three geometric hypotheses in the Lean metric germ. Strict convexity also gives the required local exterior continuations at tangential boundary directions. [Bohr’s question, slide 11](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/bohr/linz22_twistor.pdf#page=11) · [Paternain, Salo and Uhlmann, §§3.1, 3.4, 3.7–3.8](https://users.jyu.fi/~salomi/lecturenotes/GIP2D_driver.pdf) · [Global disk isometry, Corollary A.2](https://sigma-journal.com/2026/045/sigma26-045.pdf#page=36) · [Metric hypotheses](TransportOkaGrauert/SimpleMetricGerm.lean)

Equations (2.7)–(2.8) of Bohr, Monard and Paternain give the fibre coordinate and the two differential operators. Bohr and Paternain’s Lemma 4.9 identifies their common distribution with the intrinsic transport distribution. The Lean charts have two components annihilated by these operators and an invertible real derivative. They therefore give holomorphic charts for the original complex structure, with holomorphic local inverses. Setting the base open set to the entire disk in the Lean theorem answers Bohr’s question. The classical global coordinate and intrinsic-distribution identifications are the external steps in this deduction. [Coordinate equations](https://sigma-journal.com/2026/045/sigma26-045.pdf#page=11) · [Intrinsic identification, Lemma 4.9](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jep.231.pdf#page=23) · [Lean field equivalence](TransportOkaGrauert/SteinTwistorCoordinates.lean) · [Lean chart requirements](TransportOkaGrauert/ChartSteinAtlas.lean) · [Lean theorem](TransportOkaGrauert/GermStein.lean)

Bohr, Monard and Paternain obtain global blow-downs for constant-curvature models and nearby metrics and local results for general metrics. Their Question 3.7 asks a separate question about a particular global blow-down. An independent public problem list still marked the precise Stein question partial in its 30 October 2026 review. ## Explicit model cases

For every constant conformal factor and every open base subdomain, Lean constructs a complex manifold on the physical interior and proves the three ordinary Stein properties. A second unconditional theorem does the same for every Poincaré factor `σ(z) = −log(1−c²|z|²)`, with `0 < c < 1`. Lean computes its Gaussian curvature as `−4c²` on the closed unit disk. The combined certificate uses one globally smooth representative of the metric, proves strict boundary convexity, verifies two-sided escape and absence of conjugate points, and establishes all three Stein properties for that same metric. For this family the two explicit endpoint functions map open velocity disks into the open unit disk. Their boundary values and the base-centred divisors control both ways of tending to the boundary. ## The divisor argument

The full simple-disk theorem uses the interior form of the scalar adjoint geodesic X-ray range theorem. Given a smooth complex-valued base function (a), it asks for a smooth angular function (h) constant along the geodesic flow such that the average of (h(x,θ)) over the unit circle at (x) is (a(x)). Pestov and Uhlmann prove smooth surjectivity of (I^*) in Theorem 1.4. Their adjoint integrates the invariant extension of the incoming boundary data over each unit circle. The formalization uses the probability normalization of the angular measure. Rescaling the data by the total circle mass and applying the real theorem to the real and imaginary parts gives the stated normalization for complex functions. [Pestov and Uhlmann, Theorem 1.4 and section 3](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v161-n2-p12.pdf) · [Interior range statement and checked implication to both exact endpoints](TransportOkaGrauert/SteinMomentBridge.lean)

Lean proves the following components uniformly for every conformal factor satisfying the geometric definition of simplicity.

1. The geodesic flow extends smoothly and depends smoothly on the initial state and time. The disk has a slightly larger simple extension. The geodesic exponential gives global smooth normal coordinates on the open disk about each interior centre. Applying this to the enlargement constructs the radial fields for every centre of the original **closed** disk and proves the Hessian and connection equations. [Smooth ODE flow](TransportOkaGrauert/GlobalGeodesicFlow.lean) · [Simple enlargement](TransportOkaGrauert/SimpleEnlargement.lean) · [Exponential and inverse](TransportOkaGrauert/SimpleRadialSpray.lean) · [Radial pencil data](TransportOkaGrauert/SimpleRadialGeometry.lean)
2. Every smooth angular source admits a smooth solution of the unrestricted transport equation. Lean constructs a smooth time function using backward escape and integrates the source along the actual flow. A separate construction takes the nonnegative Fourier modes of any smooth angular function and realizes them as an ambient smooth function holomorphic in each velocity disk. It agrees with the Cauchy integral near zero velocity and has a smooth extension across the unit circle. [Unrestricted transport](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarGeneralTransport.lean) · [Joint disk projection](TransportOkaGrauert/HardyDisk.lean)
3. Applying the scalar invariant mean theorem, Lean corrects the Fourier projection, constructs an invariant function with a simple fibre zero, and uses division at that zero to obtain the fibre-holomorphic primitive required by the pencils. The enlargement and a smooth cutoff convert a solution on the **interior** of the larger disk into the ambient representative used by the formal solver. [Reduction to one angular moment](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarGeneralMeanSolver.lean) · [Regularity transfer and final implication](TransportOkaGrauert/SteinMomentBridge.lean)

From the scalar solution and radial data Lean constructs both corrected functions. It proves the transport Cauchy–Riemann equations throughout each open fibre, verifies the differential at the centre, follows the geodesic to its first boundary hit, and uses reciprocal divisors to exclude each omitted point from the closure of a compact hull. The proof also handles two velocities over the same base point. Lean cuts it off outside a smaller collar, shortens the two escaping geodesic segments inside the collar and transports the Stein conclusions back to the original metric. For an abstract Riemannian surface equipped with the isothermal chart and the simple conformal metric, Lean constructs the normalized tangent map and pulls back the complex atlas to the actual unit tangent disk. The existence of the oriented global isothermal chart and its identification with the independently defined intrinsic transport distribution use the classical theorems cited above. ## The scalar X-ray theorem

Lean proves that a smooth real function supported on the closed disk vanishes if its integral on every boundary-to-boundary geodesic vanishes. The proof uses a smooth transport primitive, the weighted Liouville integral, the Pestov identity and the strict Jacobi estimate. For the adjoint range, Lean proves that it suffices to solve a spatial integral equation on each smaller disk inside a larger simple disk. The lifted invariant is the integral of the source over a segment that exits at both ends. Its angular average has the exact probability normalization. The curved one-sided ray kernel is smooth off the diagonal and has leading coefficient $e^{σ(x)}/|x-y|$ in Euclidean coordinates before angular normalization. [Scalar injectivity](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarPestovEstimate.lean) · [Inversion condition and both Stein conclusions](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarNormalLift.lean) · [Geodesic kernel](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarGeneralKernel.lean) · [Joint off-diagonal regularity](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarGeneralKernelJoint.lean)

For its range proof, Lean constructs the transverse ray operator $W$ and identifies its action on smooth test functions with integration against a smooth kernel on each compact product inside the disk. In geodesic polar coordinates, integration by parts in the initial angle moves the derivative off the input. A Jacobi-field identity removes the apparent singularity on the diagonal. A cutoff $χ$ equal to one on the target disk gives a compact operator $L f=χW(χf)$ on the real disk $L^2$. A Fourier-mode calculation constructs an invariant angular function with prescribed mean $f+L^2 f$ on the target disk. [Geodesic kernel identity](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarGeneralWIdentity.lean) · [Cutoff correction](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarWCutoff.lean) · [Compact kernel operator](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarDiskKernel.lean)

Scalar ray injectivity shows that any smooth vector orthogonal to all attainable means must vanish. Since both $L$ and its adjoint take all of $L^2$ into smooth functions, the compact-operator argument rules out the entire remaining cokernel. This realizes each smooth real function, and hence each smooth complex function, as the mean of an invariant trace on the smaller disk. Enlargement transfers this local result to the original closed disk. The final theorems use this proof directly. [Annihilator](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarReachableHilbert.lean) · [Compact alternative](TransportOkaGrauert/CompactNormalAlternative.lean) · [Range argument](TransportOkaGrauert/ScalarWRangeBridge.lean) · [Universal conclusions](TransportOkaGrauert/GeneralStein.lean)

## The independent exhaustion argument

The formalization constructs a positive strict Jacobi weight from geometric entrance and flow data, computes the exterior derivative defining the Levi form in the conformal frame, and obtains uniform positivity on a polar collar. The gluing theorem uses a smooth regularized maximum, proves compactness of sublevels, and gives strict positivity outside a compact set when its physical-chart branch inequalities hold. [Smooth Jacobi weight](TransportOkaGrauert/SmoothJacobiFamily.lean) · [Frame computation](TransportOkaGrauert/FrameLevi.lean) · [Polar CR comparison](TransportOkaGrauert/PolarCR.lean) · [Uniform polar collar](TransportOkaGrauert/PolarCollar.lean) · [Gluing](TransportOkaGrauert/CollarGluing.lean)

For every simple conformal disk, Lean constructs a smooth function on the full open transport bidisk whose sublevels are compact and whose Levi form is positive definite at every point. The Jacobi weight gives the velocity-boundary collar and strict boundary convexity gives the base collar. A local potential controls the compact region left by collar gluing, and a regularized maximum joins the functions. Lean checks the physical domain, the actual $dd^c$ and the complex-linearity condition defining the holomorphic tests. On the integrable transport complex manifold, Grauert’s solution of the Levi problem gives ordinary Steinness. The separate divisor argument proves the global holomorphic-function conclusions for every open base subdomain without invoking Grauert’s theorem. ## Reproduce

The source archive includes the Lean modules, the Lake manifest, the version of Lean, the audit code and a SHA-256 manifest. In a freshly extracted copy, run these commands from the directory containing `lakefile.toml`. With dependencies already installed, the second command suffices.

```sh
lake exe cache get
./scripts/check-stein.sh
```

The script builds the library, verifies coverage of the project modules and audits their transitive axioms, replays the project's compiled declarations through Lean's kernel module by module, and checks both the universal chart statement and its ordinary complex-manifold conclusion. The flat and negative-curvature control theorems exercise the same detector. Replay uses the imported Lean and mathlib environments. The allowed axioms are propositional extensionality, classical choice and quotient soundness. A dependency on a proof placeholder fails the axiom audit. An additional hypothesis changes the type and fails the final theorem check. The final step requires exact declarations for both universal conclusions.

The release acceptance test checks the exact types of both universal theorems. Its output and the source verification record identify the scope of the run. [Test result](output/verification/stein-endpoints.txt) · [Source verification](output/verification/stein-clean-build.txt)

The independent command `lake env lean ReplayProjectTogether.lean` starts from upstream dependencies alone and gives Lean's kernel the full table of project declarations. It also checks that both named results occur in the rebuilt kernel environment. The two compiler-generated runtime recursion helpers omitted by the logical replay are named in the output.
